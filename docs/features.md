# Features

## AI Coach & Conversational Intelligence
- **Natural Language Coaching**: Conversational coaching without rigid trigger words, supporting natural phrasing, corrections, follow-up questions, and pronoun resolution across turns.
- **Selectable model provider**: OpenAI Responses (or a compatible endpoint) or Claude through the Anthropic Messages API, chosen under Coach & Modell, both with real-time SSE token streaming. Chat Completions and fallback between providers are unsupported.
- **Durable Turn Queueing**: Every chat request is persisted in a durable SQLite background queue before processing, enabling seamless answer recovery across network drops or browser reloads.
- **Permanent Fact Memorization**: Conversational profile updates save athlete preferences, equipment notes, and constraints to the durable profile when the athlete explicitly asks in Coach Chat; no separate UI confirmation is needed.
- **Sport-Specific Training Preferences**: The profile keeps general training preferences plus separate preferences for running, cycling, swimming, strength training, and mobility; the Coach saves sport-specific wishes in the matching field.
- **Training Model**: The profile selects the intensity distribution the Coach plans with: pyramidal (default), polarized, threshold-focused or sweet spot. The Coach changes it only when the athlete asks, with a change receipt and undo.
- **Calendar Notes**: Timed calendar events never block a plan write. A hard or long unit that overlaps a timed event or falls on a day with at least eight hours of events gets a `calendar_load` note, and one on a day with an all-day event a soft `all_day_event` note; the planning brief lists all-day events per date with their training-impact flags and never subtracts them. The planning brief is capped at 20,000 characters by dropping whole entries with an `omitted` note: far-future all-day and busy-minute days first, then planned sessions, planned distribution and strength weeks from the end, and the oldest completed weeks last. The profile holds no weekly plan; weekday times come from the stated availability and the calendar.
- **Server-Side Write Gate**: Every Coach write tool call is checked by impact, not by wording.
  In a turn started by the athlete's own message, reversible local edits run directly and appear under the answer as a receipt row with a check icon and a direct "Rückgängig" where fully tracked; the row is followed by one line per changed record (for example the old and new duration) and, where there is no undo, by the reason; a removal that was not confirmed shows "Löschen nicht ausgeführt – bitte bestätigen".
  A failed Coach turn (provider timeout, exhausted credit, …) shows its error at the reply itself, in a status style that stays on older replies, with "Erneut senden" (which sends the athlete's text again as a new turn) or, for a key, balance or model problem, "Anbindungen öffnen"; a stopped answer reads "Abgebrochen" with the same retry, and the error names the selected provider the same way the status line does; the global error toast is only for failures that are not shown in the chat.
  Deleting or archiving records, replacing or committing a whole plan, and changing more than 7 records in one turn (a failed call counts none, so its corrected retry needs no extra confirmation) additionally need a small model classifier to confirm that the athlete explicitly asked for exactly that in the same data area (a requested deletion of a meal never confirms deleting a race); otherwise, or when the classifier fails, the Coach asks one clarification question and nothing is changed.
  Undoing a creation deletes the record, so the Coach offers it as a confirmation card instead of running it directly.
  Intervals.icu writes need the athlete's message (or a short "ja" to the Coach's closing sync question) to name the sync with Intervals.icu or a sync verb, so planning words like calendar or conflict alone do not count, and a refusal ("nicht synchronisieren", "mache ich selbst") blocks the whole message; the classifier can never authorize them.
  A named sync runs without an approval card, and its receipt names each remote deletion it carries.
  Adaptive replans keep their preview approval: only the athlete's very next message after the published preview can apply it, and the classifier must confirm that this message accepts the previewed change as proposed (a bare "danke", an unrelated message, or a rejection that asks for a different change instead does not); an illness-pause sync to Intervals.icu additionally needs the named sync and is built from the stored preview.
  A turn makes at most 25 changes.
  Plan writes by the Coach (patch, change set, replace, library schedule, commit, and the approved adaptive replan, which is rolled back as a whole on a block) are also checked on the resulting plan: a run more than 10 % longer (distance) than the longest run of the last 30 days, a planned run or ride whose load (duration × intensity², from the workout steps against the thresholds in the Coach precedence: locally confirmed zones first, then the current provider values) is more than 10 % (rides 30 %) above the highest-load session of its sport in 30 days, a planned weekly run or ride load more than 20 % above the peak of the previous 3-4 weeks, hard run/ride sessions on consecutive days, heavy strength within 6 h of a hard session, no taper before an A or B race, and hard or 90+ minute sessions within 5 days (today included) of a reported illness, or sessions above the current stage of the return after an approved illness pause (today and tomorrow),
  plus a note when four consecutive weeks each hold more run and ride time than the week before (no lighter week; a ride load is named only from 30 %, and the weekly rise is a planning convention, no ratio, #3918),
  a taper before an A race being final 14 days of run and ride time at least 20 % lighter with a hard session in each final week, before a B race only lighter last 5 days (#3919),
  plus a note when a planned week's low/moderate/high time (from the workout steps, run and ride only; a unit without parseable steps counts by its planned intensity, else by its session focus, marked approximate; other unknown time reported as uncovered) lies clearly outside the reference range of the chosen training model,
  and leg strength (legs, full body, plyometrics; only the number and intensity of sessions count, no tonnage or load): heavy leg strength less than 48 h or moderate less than 24 h before a hard or long run/ride, more than 3 leg sessions or more than 2 heavy ones in a week, or none in a plan of 2+ weeks when the profile or season phase expects strength.
  A strength unit the Coach creates, moves or changes without `strength_structure`, `session_focus` and `strength_intensity` is also rolled back (`strength_incomplete`, never acceptable; a run or ride unit without `session_focus` is blocked the same way by `focus_missing`; untouched older units do not block other writes).
  Of the training rules only the illness rule blocks: such a write is rolled back and the findings return so the Coach corrects the plan, and only an explicit athlete wish lets it resubmit with `accept_plan_findings`, which is shown in the change receipt (the wish is asked only when the write actually produced the illness finding).
  The running week is judged as "completed + planned": its completed days count with the remaining planned days for the intensity distribution and the leg strength limit.
  The other rules rest on observational or practice-based evidence, so the plan is saved and they come back as notes with their evidence strength (a run or load spike of 10-30 % is a notice, beyond 30 % a warning) shown with dates and evidence in the change receipt.
  Completed load uses ride Normalized Power over FTP and run grade-adjusted pace over threshold pace, with a heart-rate fallback marked as less precise; "hard" comes from the unit's session focus ("Art der Einheit"), else the computed intensity, with text and keywords only as fallback.
  Load notes are extrapolated from distance/volume studies, not directly validated; missing or stale (no current provider value; local values do not age) thresholds or unparsed steps give a "Last unbekannt" note instead of a number.
  Strength takes no part because planned units carry no session-RPE.
  The Coach can justify them without repeating the receipt.
  Edits made by the athlete in the UI are not checked.
  Weekly and block plan requests get a `planning_brief` (last weeks per sport, short deterministic `lessons` from the last block review, planned intensity distribution, planned strength sessions by leg and intensity, longest run, next races, `training_model`, and the season phase (base, build, peak, taper, transition) with the weeks to the next A race, derived read-only from competition dates, with transition for 28 days after an A race; unknown without such a race; B and C races do not set the phase, #3919).
  The plan playbook sets the emphasis per phase for plan_week, plan_block and adjust_week.
- **Request-Specific Context Projection**: Compact daily, activity and dialogue windows for routine turns; detailed local context and additional tools are available on demand, with conservative fallback for continuations.
- **Targeted Clarification Protocol**: Ambiguous coaching prompts generate a single, concrete clarification question stored locally across reloads and model switches until answered.
- **Atomic Training Changesets**: Plan updates, session moves, and workout creations use transactional revision tracking and object hashes to prevent race conditions.
- **Contextual Quick Actions**: Buttons for the Morning Check-in and an "Analyse & Plan" menu that fill the composer with the request (nothing is sent until the athlete sends it): analyse the latest session (code computes every check, such as plan against actual, decoupling, fade across reps or recovery heart-rate drop, with its value, threshold, basis and verdict as the `session_report` of `get_activity_details`; the Coach explains it, leads with the most severe notable checks and names unknown ones with their reason), review progress over 4/8/12 weeks or since the last test (one review that leads with performance at equal effort: output per heart beat of steady sessions and pace at a fixed heart-rate band, and names plan execution and subjective signals only as possible causes; descriptive and read-only), plan the next week, plan a 2–4 week block, or adjust the current week to what happened.
  Each action carries its own request kind, so the server adds the matching context and a sourced training-science playbook (intensity distribution from the profile training model, hard/easy spacing, strength with endurance, taper) to that turn only.
  Plans may combine running, cycling and strength, and schedule a run critical-speed test and a Zwift FTP ramp test about every 6 to 8 weeks; the Coach evaluates a test against the last one of the same protocol and recommends threshold updates without writing them.
- **Session Bundling**: Dual-recorded workouts (e.g., Wahoo and Garmin cycling files) are bundled into one session automatically; the provider copies stay stored and nothing is deleted at the provider.
- **Structured Error Recovery**: Robust recovery from provider rate limits and transient network errors with transparent diagnostic reporting and safe rollback of incomplete actions.

## Training Calendar & Workout Planning
- **Keyboard Shortcuts**: On a keyboard, Alt+1 to Alt+5 open Coach, Kalender, Analyse, Ernährung and Mehr, never while typing in a field or behind a dialog; Mehr › App lists them under "Tastenkürzel" on wide screens.
- **Calendar Navigation**: The `Kalender` tab combines planned and completed training. `Analyse` contains performance metrics in Laufen, Radfahren, Kraft, Belastung and Erholung (Laufen and Radfahren end with their records) (all-time bests and single-activity records; the Coach reads the same records, #3826, #3831; Kraft: sessions per week and the top set per exercise from the stored sets, #3764); Laufen and Radfahren list recurring sessions with identical targets, the values the Coach reads, read after the profile cards (the pace at equal heart rate is the Coach's only; its history is the LT1 line in Entwicklung, #3153); single sessions open in the calendar card with "Details"; paging back has no limit, and weeks before the shown range load their sessions on demand; a sport filter narrows the shown sessions (see Weekly Calendar).
- **Weekly Calendar**: The calendar opens on the current week as a timeline (weekday and date column, today filled) with previous/next week buttons and the ISO week, within four weeks ahead; the week before loads at once, older weeks on demand (a fixed range, #3914).
  Below a past or running week of the last 52 (this one and the 51 before), a "Wochenrückblick" card shows that week's review in two columns of tiles, always open (#3298, moved from Analyse; old `#analysis/week` links open the calendar): Plan (completed of planned, with missed or still open units), Einheiten (with the sessions outside the plan), Zeit and Belastung (with the change against the week before once the week has ended), Schlaf Ø (with its change and source), Ruheherzfrequenz Ø and HRV Ø (each with its position against the personal range then and its source), Check-ins (with the score means), Besondere Einheiten (full width) and Beschwerden; a tile without data is left out, a week without any data and a future week show no card, and a running week reads "bis heute". It follows the week stepper and replaces the plan line under the week, which any other week keeps; a sport filter hides both, as they count the whole week.
  With structured complaints the Beschwerden tile spans the full width with one line per body area and side, such as "Achillessehne rechts · 3 Tage · Stärke 3 → 5" (a worsening in red), then the pain days told only in words and the illness days (#3766).
  A read that fails or times out never removes the card: it shows "Wochenrückblick gerade nicht verfügbar" with "Erneut laden", and the next calendar refresh retries (#3391).
  One compact row per session (sport icon, title, duration, distance (a planned unit counted in distance shows the sum of its steps); a zone bar only for a planned workout profile; completed and missed sessions are one slim row with time and status such as "✓ Absolviert · 101 %", "✓ Ungeplant" or "✕ Nicht absolviert"; activity names show without the athlete's own place, the profile's weather location, as in "Emsdetten - ") and a fixed planned/completed grid for duration, distance and load.
  Planned sessions are dashed, completed ones solid and missed ones outlined in red; identical extra activities of a day share one row, and workout profiles come with a zone legend. An opened unit has its zone key under its profile and, for a completed session, one accent action "Mit Coach besprechen" beside "Alle Details" and the quiet "Feedback"; the card header closes it, and a done unit shows no focus field (#4129, #4221).
  Opening a session shows its details; on wide screens they appear as a detail pane beside the week, with a larger zone profile, and one session of the week is open from the start.
  A planned session lists its steps (zone, duration and the target range from the zones of its day, such as "110–128 bpm") instead of the workout notation.
  A planned run or ride of at least an hour, today or later, shows "Verpflegung" when opened: drinking per hour and in total from the athlete's own sweat rate (the median of Garmin's sweat-loss estimates of the last year for the same sport and setting; outdoors only sessions within ±5 °C of the forecast day temperature when at least 3 exist), keeping the loss below 2 % of body weight and never above the sweat rate (a loss within 2 % shows "nicht nötig" with the expected loss), with 0.5–0.7 g sodium per litre from 2 h (ACSM 2007, NATA 2017), or the starting range 0.4–0.8 l/h without own data; and carbohydrates per hour by the same steps as the race plan.
  "Verschieben" on a planned unit offers the next seven days (a day an appointment or another entry blocks is marked with its name and cannot be tapped; the library's "Einplanen" strip does the same) and "…" for a later date, both up to the last day the calendar shows; one tap moves the local unit (a unit already sent then waits for its sync), and the receipt offers "Rückgängig". A refusal names the appointment in the way and, when it still waits for its classification, offers "Termin einordnen" (the same in the library strip).
  the trash icon at the end of an opened planned unit's actions ("Einheit löschen") deletes the local unit at once (with a copy at Intervals.icu or Garmin the receipt adds that the next sync removes it) without the Coach and without a remote write, and the day shows a receipt with "Rückgängig".
  "Mit Coach besprechen" opens the Coach with the planned session named in the message field for the athlete's own question; nothing is sent until the athlete sends it.
  A "Woche | Monat" switch shows the month grid (one dot per session, on wide screens its duration and the week's time, done plus still planned); under the grid a line gives the month's done and planned time and a legend names the marks, including a blocked day; tapping a day shows its week below. A planned unit that collides with an appointment names it ("Ein Termin an diesem Tag erlaubt kein Training"), and a derived kind carries the tooltip "≈ vom Inhalt der Einheit abgeleitet" and is set with "Als Art setzen".
  Chips "Alle | Lauf | Rad | Kraft" below the switch show one sport in the week and month (a done session by its recorded sport); the week figures and month hours then count only that sport, the whole-week plan compliance line is left out, appointments stay, the week sync still covers all units, and the choice is remembered in this browser only.
  The week's count ("16 abgeschlossen · 2 geplant") and its Geplant/Absolviert totals stand under the week, in the "Wochenrückblick" when the week has one (#4308). On a phone the week starts high: the page heading "Kalender" gives way and its refresh button sits at the end of the "Woche | Monat" row, and the sport chips scroll in one row; the month view and the other calendar areas keep the heading.
  The calendar always opens on the week.
  Calendar appointments and markers are neutral day notes; a planned session that conflicts with them shows one conflict notice whose "Mit Coach besprechen" action puts the question for an adjustment into the Coach composer.
  "Ungeplant" marks extra activities only in weeks with a plan. The planned load of a week shows only when every planned unit has one (else "–" and the count of units without), changes name their reference ("ggü. Vorwoche"), and under a sport filter the week's figures under the week say "Gefiltert: <sport>". "Ruhetag geplant" shows only on a day without any session. The week footer names one load figure ("Belastung 40 von 85" once a unit is done, else "Belastung 85"). A strength line names no reps or weight it does not have. A competition card shows a start time only when one is set, a goal as a duration only when written as h:mm:ss, and "Nicht an Intervals.icu gesendet" for a local competition.
  Illness and pain from saved check-ins appear as day notices; days without sessions remain visible.
  Today shows a "Check-in" row (a pencil, "Check-in ändern", once one is stored) that opens the check-in form: the day (today, or an earlier day by its date, never a later one), day status, soreness, stress and motivation as 0–10 sliders (a slider without a stored value reads "–" and is saved only once touched; its trash icon takes an answer back, #4306), available time, the day tags, a row "Krank oder Beschwerden" (collapsed unless the day has any) with the illness symptoms of the infection hint (any symptom means rest only), illness text and structured complaints, and a note. It saves through the Coach's `save_checkin` use case, sends only what changed, and its receipt offers "Rückgängig" (#3953).
  Matched sessions show their share of the plan (load, duration or strength sets) in the status; opened, a completed session shows its chart and each value once per sport (runs: Pace, Ø Herzfrequenz, Schrittfrequenz, Ø Leistung, Belastung, RPE, Bodenkontaktzeit, Höhenmeter; rides: Normalized Power, Ø Leistung, Intensitätsfaktor, Ø Herzfrequenz, Trittfrequenz, Belastung, RPE, Ø Geschwindigkeit, Höhenmeter; other sports: Belastung, RPE, Intensität, Ø Herzfrequenz, Ø Leistung, Höhenmeter; a value without data is left out), one slim bar of the time in zones (power zones for rides that have them, else heart-rate zones) with the minutes per zone and the zone source, the plan line, "Mit Coach besprechen" and "Details"; additional activities have no invented target percentage.
  When the calendar pairs a run or ride with a planned unit, "Alle Details" (the session dialog) starts with "Soll/Ist": the share of time inside each planned step's target (laps pair with the work steps, the steps between share the time between them, a steady run shares its whole time; heart rate over the step's second half, stops left out; green from 80 %, amber from 50 %, red below), a strip of the steps sized by duration, the "Zieltreue" score (time in target over all scored time), planned against actual minutes per zone in the plan's target metric, and each step's target band dashed on its pace, heart-rate or power chart.
- **Configurable Planning Horizon**: User-adjustable calendar display settings controlling past lookback and future planning horizons through the More tab.
- **Plan vs. Actual Pairing**: Intelligent pairing of planned workouts to completed activities using provider pairing IDs with a conservative same-day sport fallback.
- **Visual Volume Comparisons**: Accurate plan-versus-actual volume matching evaluated by training load (TSS) when available, falling back to moving or elapsed duration.
- **Unmatched Activity Display**: Prominent display of completed, unscheduled sessions alongside planned workouts without fabricating missing target metrics.
- **Full Workout Lifecycle Management**: Planned workouts are scheduled, moved, edited, archived, restored, and deleted through the Coach, each change with a receipt and undo. The calendar moves and deletes a unit and offers the session focus, the Garmin exercise choice and the sync button; the missing sides are listed in [UI and Coach parity](ui-coach-parity.md).
- **Target Period Scoping**: Conversational plan adjustments strictly scoped to requested date ranges, leaving surrounding weeks and existing training blocks untouched.
- **Daily Athlete Check-ins**: Stored check-ins track soreness, stress, motivation, session RPE, day form, illness, pain, available minutes, availability notes, free-form notes, a day status (unknown, rest, pause), the tags travel, late meal and high stress, and structured complaints (#3766): per body area of a fixed list (Fuß, Sprunggelenk, Achillessehne, Wade, Schienbein, Knie, Oberschenkel vorne/hinten, Hüfte, Leiste, unterer Rücken, oberer Rücken/Nacken, Schulter, Arm/Ellbogen, Hand/Handgelenk, Sonstiges) the side and a severity 0-10, beside the free pain text.
  A body area and side reported on 3 of the last 14 days, or 2 severity points worse there, gives the planning brief and the plan checks a non-blocking note (`complaint_recurring`); these are product rules, not medical limits, and only illness blocks a plan. They are recorded through the Coach chat and feed readiness, planning, and calendar day notices. Saved check-ins stay in the local data and in exports.
- **Early Infection Hint**: A non-blocking note on today's calendar day when at least two of last night's breathing rate, resting heart rate and HRV are clearly outside the athlete's own range (method M14, #3762). "Ich bin krank" expands into symptom chips and "Bestätigen", and records illness through the check-in path with the selected symptoms, receipt and undo (#3919); "Mir geht's gut" hides the note for that day (#3821).
- **Post-Activity Feedback**: Dedicated local feedback logging for completed workouts, capturing perceived exertion (RPE), equipment details, and workout execution notes.
  "Feedback" on a completed session opens its own sheet "Wie war die Einheit?" ("Alle Details" opens the session dialog, which shows the session only): one row of taps 1 to 10 for the effort (a tap saves at once with "Rückgängig"; the watch's value is dashed and a tap takes it over; the pressed step again removes it), the same for the feel (five taps, 1 very strong to 5 very weak, stored per session), the note (saved when the field is left) and under "Mehr angeben" "Art der Einheit" and "Abweichung vom Plan". There is no save button (#4199).
  Feedback belongs to the canonical session and is stored under the local session it was written for (equipment assignments likewise), so splitting a bundle keeps it with that copy: the provider copies of one confirmed session show and edit the same feedback, and its session RPE counts once.
  Older feedback saved on different copies stays stored; the newest is shown and the others are listed as `conflicting_feedback`.
  Clearing removes the session's feedback entirely.
  An id that matches activities of several providers stays on its own, and deleting a provider copy keeps the feedback.
  Local session RPE is never mixed with the provider's RPE.
  The RPE is chosen from 0 to 10 in half steps, with the CR-10 anchors (Ruhe, Leicht, Moderat, Schwer, Maximal and others) on their steps; the time of each new or changed rating is stored, earlier ratings keep an unknown rating time.
  **Session-RPE load** (method `session-rpe-load` v2, evidence card `docs/performance-methods/m1-session-rpe-load.md`) is elapsed minutes × session RPE in arbitrary units (AU): the activity details show it per session (for example "403 AU · RPE 6,5 (Athletenfeedback) × 62 min"), Belastung shows it per day or week with the count of rated sessions, and the Coach reads it in the training report.
  Without a local rating the Garmin Connect watch RPE of the same session is used and labelled; disagreeing copies of one provider, a missing rating or a session with only a moving time give no value; a session whose elapsed time exceeds its moving time by more than 15 minutes or 20 % keeps its elapsed value and is marked as a long interruption.
  AU are never added to Intervals.icu or Garmin load.
  The same form marks what the session was (**Art der Einheit**: Training, Wettkampf, Pendeln, Einfahren, Ausfahren, or "Automatisch" for no marking).
  The marking is local and wins over Garmin's event type and over the race hint of a stored competition on every read (summaries, Coach, power-profile and critical-power efforts, plan compliance, race results): a marked race is linked to the competition of its day like a Garmin race, any other marking, training included, takes the session out of the race hint and removes its competition link (a training marking also keeps the other sessions of that day from taking the hint; mark the right one as race), and commute, Einfahren and Ausfahren sessions stay out of bests, curves and planned-session matching.
  The marking shares the receipt, undo and merge groups of the feedback rows, so undo restores the previous marking and link; deleting the notes and rating (also through the Coach) keeps the marking, and an activity that cannot be assigned to one session cannot be marked.
  The Coach sets it with `save_activity_feedback` (`purpose`, `null` removes it) only for what the athlete names.
  Saving or removing feedback in the activity details shows a receipt with "Rückgängig"; undo restores every stored row of the session as it was, full-length notes and hidden older entries included.
  Feedback keeps the session it was written for, so splitting wrongly merged sessions stays lossless.
- **Provider copy corrections**: The activity details list the provider copies bundled into a session under "Aufzeichnungen". The athlete can confirm the bundle, split one copy into its own session, or merge it with another session of the same day; the Coach can do the same on request. "Aktivität löschen" removes the activity with all its copies locally after a confirmation; a later provider delete or a new delivery of a copy does not bring it back. Each correction shows a receipt with "Rückgängig", and corrected sessions are never re-bundled by later syncs. A sync splits a copy off an unconfirmed session when it no longer matches, for example after an edit at the provider. Nothing is changed at Intervals.icu, Garmin or Wahoo.
- **Multi-Phase Season Periodization**: Long-term seasonal planning mapping base, build, peak, and taper phases (completed after the race) anchored to competition dates; a transition phase follows an A race for 28 days; only A races set the phase (#3919).

## Workout Library & Structured Formats
- **Local Workout Library**: Repository, sorted by sport and name, of reusable endurance and strength workout templates stored entirely in the local encrypted database.
- **Planning Only From the Library**: Every unit the Coach plans comes from a library template (`template_id`) and keeps its exercises or steps in order; a variant only scales sets, reps, loads, durations and targets, and the receipt names it as a scaled variant. Without a fitting template the Coach creates one first, in the same turn for a clearly described unit, and asks only when the unit is unclear. A Coach write that breaks this is rolled back (`template_missing`, `template_mismatch`); older units without a template can still be moved, archived or deleted, but their content changes only by switching them to a template. The expanded unit shows "Vorlage: <name>".
- **Template Lifecycle Management**: Reusable workout templates are created, modified, scheduled, archived, and deleted through the Coach; the library view lists, schedules and archives them (missing sides: [UI and Coach parity](ui-coach-parity.md)).
  An opened library card also offers "Einplanen" (the day strip of "Verschieben") and "Archivieren" that archives the entry at once (the "Archiviert" filter offers "Wiederherstellen" and the final, red "Löschen"); both work without the Coach, write nothing remotely and show a receipt with "Rückgängig".
  An archived template that was never synchronized is deleted for good 30 days after archiving (athlete decision 2026-11-15); until then "Rückgängig" and the Coach restore bring it back, and synced templates stay archived.
- **Strict Endurance Syntax Enforcement**: Rigorous validation of endurance steps requiring explicit duration or distance alongside defined intensity targets (e.g., `- 15m 50-70%` or `- 6km Z1 HR`).
- **Relative Target Calculations**: Automatic resolution of percentage-based targets against the athlete's current FTP, threshold heart rate, or threshold pace (local, Garmin Connect or Wahoo).
- **Native Repeat Block Formatting**: Standardized parsing for interval repeat blocks with mandatory preceding count headers, blank-line delimitation, and step-level recovery tracking.
- **Prose Support for Non-Endurance Workouts**: Flexible free-form text formatting for strength training, yoga, mobility, and core routines without rigid step constraints.
- **Provider Upload Verification**: Two-phase upload verification ensuring Intervals.icu parses step instructions, duration, and training load before marking a workout synced.
- **Rejection of Ambiguous Formats**: Pre-upload validation rejecting prose-only endurance entries, mismatched target types, and conflicting watt conversions.

## Intervals.icu Output
- **Output-only Intervals.icu**: Intervals.icu receives library workouts, indoor and virtual ride units (for Zwift), competitions and sport settings only on the athlete's explicit synchronization. Its activities, wellness history and activity details are not read, and no setting turns that on; the app only reads back records that an explicit export wrote. The Intervals.icu card (More → Anbindungen) keeps the connection check. Release 2.0.0 removed the Intervals.icu ingest data stored by earlier releases.
- **Session Focus**: Every planned unit and library template can carry a local "Art der Einheit": run and ride recovery, easy, endurance, long, tempo, threshold, vo2max, anaerobic, race or test (ride also sweet_spot), strength legs, upper, full_body, core, plyometrics or mobility with an optional light, moderate or heavy intensity.
  The Coach sets it for every new unit, and the athlete changes it in the opened calendar entry; a derived value is offered next to its empty field as "≈ … übernehmen" and becomes set with one tap.
  A unit without focus shows a dashed, derived chip taken from its parsed steps, then from keywords such as Squats, Beintag or Schwelle.
  For strength an explicit term in the name (Oberkörper, Unterkörper/Beine, Ganzkörper, Core, Mobility) beats exercise keywords, negated mentions ("Beine schonen", "ohne Beine", "kein Beintraining", "nicht schwer") never count, and without a structure the intensity is derived from the text (schwer, moderat/mittel, leicht).
  The focus stays local: the Intervals.icu export is unchanged, and it is covered by change history, undo and backup.
- **Run Heart-Rate Caps**: Recovery, easy, endurance ("Grundlage") and long run units carry a local `hr_cap` that is stored in percent only: the cap in percent of the run threshold heart rate ("LSHF"), the lower bound of the exported range and the cap source (default, measured, or estimated).
  The bpm is never stored: wherever it is shown, checked or exported it is resolved from the threshold in effect on the unit's date (local zones, then the dated Garmin watch zone set), so an adopted threshold applies without editing a unit; without a known threshold only the percent is shown.
  The card reads "≤ <percent> % LSHF · <bpm> bpm · <source>".

  The Garmin workout and schedule ids are kept in the local remote links.

## Weather Intelligence & Outdoor Scheduling
- **Open-Meteo Integration**: Server-side cached 14-day local weather forecasts powered by the non-commercial Open-Meteo meteorological API.
- **Dual-Model Forecast Engine**: Intelligent forecast resolution using high-precision DWD ICON-D2 for short-range predictions and ECMWF IFS HRES for long-range outlooks.
- **Air Quality & Pollen**: Open-Meteo/CAMS European AQI, particulate matter, ozone, nitrogen dioxide, and UV index for about 5 days. For German locations the official DWD pollen-flight hazard index (0–3, today plus two days) is used for the automatically resolved pollen region; elsewhere Open-Meteo/CAMS pollen concentrations are the fallback.
- **Compact Calendar Weather**: Each calendar day shows only a weather symbol with its temperature range beside the date, so the sessions and their load/interval charts stay in the foreground; small flags are added only when notable (rain from 50 %, UV from 6, European AQI from 60, any DWD pollen level, CAMS pollen from 10 grains/m³), and all weather and air figures appear in the symbol's tooltip.
- **Weather Window Recommendations**: Automated calculation of optimal outdoor training windows over the next 5 days based on temperature, precipitation, wind speeds, and air quality.
- **Workday Schedule Awareness**: Outdoor suggestions use fixed clock windows, not a configurable working-hours setting: Monday-Thursday 05:00-06:00, 12:00-13:00 and 16:00-22:00, Friday 05:00-06:00, 12:00-13:00 and 14:00-22:00, Saturday and Sunday 06:00-21:00.
- **Adaptive Weather Reason**: An outdoor ride of at least 180 minutes today or in the next two days gets a reason for the adaptive replanning preview when the forecast shows persistent rain or snow. There is no heat or wind rule and no weather notification.
- **Weather During Sessions**: Each ride or run since 2022 that is outdoors, or whose environment is unknown but has a stored GPS start position, gets the Open-Meteo historical-forecast weather (temperature, apparent temperature, humidity, wind, precipitation) at its start position, or, for an outdoor session without a stored position, at the saved weather location, labelled as home; an unknown-environment session never uses the home. Only coordinates rounded to two decimals leave the server. A stored result is never fetched again; failed requests are retried later with a growing delay. The Coach sees the weather and a heat note from an apparent temperature of 25 °C, so heat is not read as a fitness change, never a coordinate.
  A session on its own track also gets the wind direction and its mean headwind component along the direction of travel (an estimate from the start cell's 10 m wind), derived in the sync stage from positions that never leave storage (#3084); a change of this method looks stored sessions up again at most 300 times per hour.
- **Route Recognition**: Each ride or run with a stored GPS track that is not known to be indoor or virtual is fingerprinted by its start, its distance and the coarse 500 m grid cells (sampled every 150 m) it passes. Sessions are the same route when their distances differ by at most 10 %, the start (or, reversed, the end) lies in or beside the other's start cell and at least 60 % of the cells (Jaccard overlap) agree; direction does not matter. Repeats are numbered by start time. The activity view and the Coach (`route` block of `get_activity_details`) get only the label ("Strecke 3" until the athlete renames it with `POST /api/routes/rename`), the visit number, the visit count, an opaque id and the visits of the route, never a coordinate or cell, so efforts on one loop are comparable.
  The session details show the route as "Strecke 9,8 km · 7. von 9 Besuchen" (an unnamed route is named by its distance, with its number added when two have the same) with "Umbenennen", a "Pace je Besuch" chart (km/h for rides) and an open "Alle 9 Besuche ansehen" table of every visit, newest first, six at a time with "Ältere Besuche" (date, moving time, average heart rate, pace or speed, the activity weather's temperature; missing values "–"; the opened session marked). The Coach's `route.history` holds the session's visit and up to five before it with the same values. Sessions without a usable track get no route.
- **Efficient Server Caching**: 3-hour server-side forecast caching with automatic on-demand cache overrides accessible from the More tab.

## External Calendars & Schedule Constraints
- **Read-Only iCalendar (ICS) Sync**: Secure polling of private external iCalendar feeds (Google Calendar, Apple iCloud, Microsoft Outlook) without write permissions.
- **Rolling 8-Week Event Horizon**: Bounded calendar expansion mapping external life events across an 8-week (56-day) forward-looking window.
- **RFC 5545 Recurrence Engine**: Comprehensive expansion of standard recurring rules (daily, weekly, monthly, yearly) and Google Calendar recurrence exceptions capped at 1,000 instances. A date-only `UNTIL` includes recurrences on that day in the event's own time zone, and feeds whose recurrence expansion is too expensive are rejected. A component with an unsupported or invalid recurrence rule or missing UID/DTSTART is skipped instead of failing the feed; the sync result and the calendar status count the skips ("n Termine übersprungen", reason class only, no event text). A series whose `UNTIL` lies before the window is dropped silently. `DURATION` accepts weeks (`P1W`); property parameters may carry quoted colons.
- **Bounded Calendar Processing**: Feeds are limited to 5 MB and 10,000 parsed event components; folded lines, RDATE deduplication and recurrence-exception lookup are processed linearly. Instance records are generated incrementally and feeds exceeding 1,000 unique events in the sync window are rejected. Connected calendar HTTP transfers have a 30-second total deadline.
- **Personal Events in the Calendar**: From today on, every event of the feed shows as a chip above the day's units with its impact: Blockiert, Kürzer, Locker, Egal or Offen (no decision and no marker yet). A tap opens a sheet (a side panel on wide screens) to set the impact for this event or, for a recurring one, for every occurrence; the decision is stored locally, survives feed refreshes, overrides the markers and changes planning like the matching marker. The month view marks days with a blocked event. Undecided events from today through 14 days ahead raise a notice: "{n} neue Termine bis {date}" above the calendar with "Einordnen", which walks through them one by one, and the same count on the Kalender navigation item; it goes away once each is decided ("Egal" counts) and never returns for an event already decided.
  The navigation count is one number: these events plus what the running week's (Monday to Sunday) "Woche synchronisieren" would send or remove, by the button's own rule, each unit once whatever the providers (#3384). Its accessible name names the non-zero parts, for example "Kalender 5, 2 neue Termine bis 01.12., 3 Einheiten zu synchronisieren" (it starts with its visible text, the name and the count).
- **Calendar Training Constraints**: `[NO_TRAINING]`, `[NO_INTENSITY]`, and `[SHORT_ONLY]` are recognized in event titles and descriptions. `[NO_TRAINING]` and `[NO_INTENSITY]` are enforced against planned-workout changes on the affected local calendar day: training is blocked for the former, the latter blocks hard workouts (the one hard-session rule shared by plan checks, adaptive replanning and race-proximity conflicts) and strength that is not explicitly easy, and `[SHORT_ONLY]` blocks workouts longer than 60 minutes.
- **Calendar-aware Coach planning**: Week and block planning include external events within 14 and 35 days and summarize occupied time without double-counting overlaps. The Coach asks whether to train or reduce intensity when an event makes the choice unclear. A long or hard session overlapping an event or falling on a day with at least eight occupied hours gets a non-blocking plan note; explicit calendar markers remain enforced.
- **Visual Schedule Conflict Markers**: Distinctive visual indicators on the planned calendar alerting the athlete to busy days and potential scheduling conflicts.
- **Adaptive Session Replanning**: Heuristic session adjustments that suggest shorter durations or lower-intensity replacements for scheduled workouts on congested days. A strength unit keeps its exercises, sets and weights and only gets the light strength intensity; a run or ride may become a generic easy replacement.
- **Stepwise Return After an Illness Pause**: After an approved pause window, three return stages follow: stage 1 easy sessions only, stage 2 easy and moderate, stage 3 the normal plan.
  Stage 1 starts the day after the pause; each stage lasts at least 2 days. After any selected symptom the return waits at stage 0, rest only, until two later check-ins have no symptom selected (#3919).
  Each morning the adaptive preview proposes at most one stage forward when the check-in of the day reports no pain or illness and a normal body feel (soreness up to 5 of 10), the overnight HRV (RMSSD) is not below the lower quartile of the athlete's own earlier nights by more than half the interquartile range (the margin of the infection hint, #3920) (the personal recovery baseline: 42 days, at least 14 earlier nights, same measurement context) and the resting heart rate is at most 5 bpm above the median of those nights.
  A poor sign holds the stage; a new pain or illness complaint steps back one stage (an illness report starts a new pause forecast instead).
  If the Garmin night is missing or has too few earlier nights, the check-in alone decides; without today's check-in the stage holds.
  A pause day in the calendar names the day the pause runs to ("Trainingspause bis …": its consecutive pause days or the approved illness pause) and offers "Mit Coach besprechen" for the way back (#3932).
  Evidence is shown per source (local check-in, Garmin).
  The preview also replaces drafts of today and tomorrow that exceed the stage (easy, at most 60 minutes), as local drafts only; nothing is written to Intervals.icu.
  A stage change applies only with the preview approval and is stored on the applied adjustment (no separate state).
  While a stage below 3 runs, the plan guard treats sessions above it like the illness rule (`illness_hard`).
  An unadvanced return ends after 28 days.

## Performance Analytics, Metrics & Trends
- **Historical Trends**: Trend lines for FTP, running threshold pace and VO2 max (4 weeks, 3, 6 or 12 months; default 3 months), for sleep, resting heart rate and heart rate variability (HRV) (default 4 weeks) and for body weight (default 3 months); every period selector of Analyse and Ernährung offers the same four periods: 4 Wochen, 3 Monate, 6 Monate and 12 Monate (#3839).
  4 Wochen draw days, longer periods calendar weeks; 3 Monate come with the state, 6 and 12 Monate read `GET /api/analysis/trend-history` once on demand (stored values only, never a provider); Erholung's 3 Monate read it too, its base read holds 84 days. The previous-year comparison (#3765) is gone.
- **Local Session Load**: Every session's TSS-scale load and intensity factor are computed locally with method M6 (`docs/performance-methods/m6-local-tss-load.md`): power against the FTP valid on that day, else run pace against the threshold pace, else heart rate as normalised Banister TRIMP (walking included) with the constants of the profile setting **Geschlecht** (male or female; not set uses the male constants, marked as an assumption in the Coach's session data).
  Strength takes its session-RPE load (elapsed minutes × RPE) times a personal TSS-per-AU factor: the median over the locally or Garmin-rated rides and runs of the last 365 days at a similar RPE (within 1 of the strength session's, else within 2), from at least 20 of them; unrated or uncalibrated strength gets none.
  Yoga and alpine ski get none.
  Provider loads (Intervals.icu, Wahoo, Garmin) are never summed.
  Values are stored per session and recomputed after each provider sync, local zone change, session correction, changed session RPE and change of a preferred threshold source or of the profile sex.
- **Impulse-Response Fitness Modeling**: Fitness, fatigue and form come only from the local curve (card M7, `docs/performance-methods/m7-local-fitness-fatigue.md`): the local TSS-scale session load smoothed with 42 and 7 days over the whole stored history, computed on read. The load tab, the Coach load block and training report, comparisons, the progress report and block review, the daily planning context and the load scenarios all read it; days whose sessions have no local load (for example unrated strength or yoga) are marked incomplete. Stored Intervals.icu CTL, ATL and form are not shown and never mixed into the local values. No ramp rate is derived.
- **Session Efficiency Metrics**: Each ride and run gets a local efficiency factor, variability index, mechanical work and aerobic decoupling (card M8, `docs/performance-methods/m8-local-session-metrics.md`), stored per session and shown in the activity detail and to the Coach. Work and decoupling need stored full-resolution streams; calories are the device estimate (Garmin, Wahoo). The values are descriptive: no fixed decoupling limit and no fitness verdict.
- **Running Dynamics and Form Drift**: With a sensor that measures them (running pod, HRM-Pro/Run strap or a watch), a run's activity detail shows the averages of ground contact time (ms), the share of it on the left foot (percent), vertical oscillation (cm), vertical ratio (percent) and step length (m); nothing shows when the device did not measure them.
  They come from the Garmin activity summary (`avgGroundContactTime`, `avgGroundContactBalance`, `avgVerticalOscillation`, `avgVerticalRatio`, `avgStrideLength`, where a 0 is "not measured") and as full-resolution series from the FIT record fields `stance_time`, `stance_time_balance`, `vertical_oscillation`, `vertical_ratio` and `step_length` (scales taken from the FIT SDK profile, not yet checked against a recorded file).
  A run of at least 45 minutes outside trails also gets a form drift check: the first and the last third are compared when their speed differs by at most 5 %, each third has measured data for at least half its time, and a ground contact time that rose by at least 5 % shows "Laufstil lässt nach".
  Vertical oscillation (+10 %) and step length (-5 %) are reported against their own thresholds without flagging the run.
  These thresholds are heuristics of this app, not validated cut-offs, and the check is no injury-risk score.
  The Coach receives the summary values and the drift result, never the series.
  In the session dialog ("Alle Details") each running-dynamics value of a run (with cadence) sits in its own "Laufdynamik" section with a verdict against the athlete's own runs at the same pace: per 15 s/km pace band (walking under 2.0 m/s, stops and gaps left out) the run's mean is set against the runs of the 84 days before it with at least 2 minutes in that band (at least 5 runs, else "zu wenig Vergleich"); the best third is "besser als sonst", the middle third "üblich", the weakest "schlechter als sonst" (shorter contact time, lower oscillation and ratio, higher cadence and a balance nearer 50 % count as better, Heiderscheit 2011; step length is only "länger" or "kürzer").
  A dot gives Garmin's published colour band of the run's mean as a second reference ("Garmin-Einordnung (Brustgurt)", limits from Garmin's EPIX manual), and a table lists the run's pace bands.
  The rating reads every stored run in the background build and appears once it finished.
- **Race Prediction Engine**: Garmin Connect race predictions for 5K, 10K, half marathon and marathon, shown in the running section.
- **Sport-Specific Intensity Distributions**: Heart rate, power and run pace zone distribution analysis across running, outdoor cycling, and indoor/virtual cycling activities; the Analysis load tab draws them as HF-, Power- and Pace-Zonen below the training focus. Reports count each session's seconds from its streams with the local zones in effect on the session day (stored with the session analysis); a session without streams shows its Garmin watch zones in a separate "(Gerät)" distribution, never added to local zone seconds. Intervals.icu zone times are not read.
- **Nutritional Intake Logging**: Integrated tracking of daily caloric intake and macronutrient splits (protein, carbohydrates, fats) stored alongside training load. A logged meal's components can be removed, resized, swapped or added ("+ Zutat", #4270) in "Mahlzeit bearbeiten", stored together with one undo.
- **Explicit Metric Freshness**: Clear differentiation between observation timestamps and synchronization retrieval times to prevent outdated metrics appearing fresh.

## Multimodal Inputs & File Attachments
- **Multi-File Attachment Support**: Seamless file upload supporting up to 4 concurrent GPX tracks, FIT files, or image files (PNG, JPEG, WebP): GPX and FIT up to 5 MB each, images up to 15 MB before the browser downsizes them (the server accepts 5 MB per decoded file).
- **Server-Side GPX Processing**: In-memory parsing of GPX tracks calculating total distance, un-smoothed elevation gain, and sampled geographic coordinates for the Coach. The Coach receives the parsed points, not the file: no author or device metadata, and no positions within 500 m of the start and end.
- **Binary FIT Activity Summaries**: Local extraction of binary FIT files summarizing elapsed time, distance, normalized power, average heart rate, and cadence. The Coach receives the parsed records, laps and sessions, not the original file, without device serial numbers, the FIT user profile or positions within 500 m of the start and end.
- **Visual Technique Analysis**: Image forwarding to multimodal AI models enabling visual evaluation of training charts, race routes, or workout screenshots, with EXIF and other metadata removed first.
- **Encrypted Attachment Storage**: Durable storage of uploaded attachments within the encrypted SQLite database, included in full backups and privacy exports.

## Privacy, Security & Data Management
- **SQLCipher AES-256 Encryption**: Complete encryption of all athlete data, metrics, database-stored provider credentials (API keys and secrets entered in the UI), provider tokens (Wahoo and Garmin), chat history, and attachments at rest using `APP_PASSWORD`. Only a legacy Garmin token file, if one still exists, is plaintext and not covered; see [setup](setup.md#garmin-authentication--mfa-setup).
- **Database Key Placement**: Keep `APP_PASSWORD` in the container environment or the project-root `.env`. If it is found in `DATA_DIR/.env`, beside the encrypted database, startup logs a warning (never the value), because a copy of the data directory would then include its key.
- **Strict Session Security**: High-security session cookies hardened with `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Strict`, and optional `Secure` flags.
- **Bounded HTTP Input**: At most 32 active handlers, with a 20-second limit for the request headers and a separate 20-second limit for each 1 MiB piece of a request body, so a slow but steady upload is not cut off. Login admission is checked before reading its body; these input limits do not shorten Coach responses or SSE streams.
- **Zero Third-Party Trackers**: Self-hosted architecture containing zero tracking scripts, third-party analytics, external CDNs, or telemetry reporting.
- **Comprehensive Privacy Export**: Single-click export producing a complete, unencrypted ZIP archive of JSON and JSON Lines files of all profile records, workouts, metrics, and chat history.
- **Confirmed Local Data Purge**: Irreversible deletion of all local athlete data, including stored provider credentials and Garmin tokens, guarded by an explicit typed confirmation phrase (`LOKALE DATEN LÖSCHEN`). The Garmin account and Intervals.icu/Wahoo accounts themselves are not changed, but the app's Wahoo grant is revoked at Wahoo first (best effort; a failure is reported and does not stop the purge).
- **Zero-Downtime Maintenance Mode**: Process-level maintenance gate that prevents concurrent writes and ensures transaction safety during database restoration.
- **Pre-Restore Rollback Copies**: Automated creation of a safety copy of the existing database before executing any database restore or replacement.
- **Redacted Operational Logging**: Structured server logs that correlate technical operation IDs while stripping authentication headers, tokens, and athlete text.
- **Always-on Technical Diagnostics**: Bounded diagnostic logger (up to 10,000 entries from the last 7 days, 2 MiB total, 512 KiB per entry) recording sanitized OpenAI request bodies, response and non-OpenAI request shapes (field names, types, lengths, no values), provider error details, tool activity, and error traces. Credentials, keys, tokens, and session data are removed before storage. New entries are written to the database in one batch at most 30 seconds after they are captured, as background work, and on shutdown, including `docker stop`; a crash or forced kill can lose the entries of the last 30 seconds.
